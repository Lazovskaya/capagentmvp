**Idea to Market (I2M)**

**Products & Services**

Person wearing mixed reality smartglasses touching transparent screen

Idea to Market covers all processes associated with managing the lifecycle of products and services, such as managing the product portfolio and investments, identifying new products and services, finalizing design through prototyping, and testing before handover to manufacturing. After production, activities associated with bringing products and services to market must be managed, including the customer experience, intellectual property, and product compliance. I2M also includes processes associated with managing changes to products or services, updating road maps, and making sure data on products and services is accessible yet secure during collaboration between internal and external communities throughout the product development process.

Products and services that are managed as part of the I2M process include assemblies, finished tangible products, intangible products, such as software and digital products, and procured products. The latter could be raw materials required for production, products that are resold to customers, MRO items, or products and services used for internal consumption, such as indirect goods and services.

The I2M process varies mainly based on the type of product being managed such as tangible or intangible, services or solutions.

It also varies based on whether the product is being produced and delivered or procured. It also varies based on whether the product is being produced or procured, services which are delivered.

**Idea to Market for Utility Retailer**

includes activities associated with managing the lifecycle of a new service for a power utility retailer. These activities encompass various stages, including defining prerequisites, conceptualizing the service offering, procuring energy sources, establishing service delivery contracts for seamless execution, and undertaking essential pre-launch Go-To-Market (GTM) activities such as pricing.

**Product and Portfolio Management (Wholesale Distribution)**

involves all activities related to managing the entire lifecycle of physical products intended for resale within the wholesale distribution industry. This entails activities such as product portfolio planning, establishing sourcing contracts, resulting in a defined product portfolio presented to customers. Additionally, it involves making decisions on product/solution bundling and pricing, as well as the management of product data and associated information throughout this process

**Idea to Release for Software**

involves all activities related to managing the lifecycle of a digital product or service, such as in the software industry. This encompasses crafting a product strategy, strategizing financial investments, formulating requirements, overseeing product design and development, preparing for operational deployment, and making decisions regarding product/service bundling and pricing

**Idea to Market for Procured Intermediate Physical Product**

involves all activities associated with identifying products and services that are to be procured and adding them to the portfolio. It includes product sampling and sourcing to ensure products and suppliers meet requirements. Products can be raw materials required for production, products that are resold to customers, assemblies and MRO items.

**Idea to Market for Produced Physical Products (discrete industry)**

includes all activities associated with managing the lifecycle of a tangible product that is manufactured in a discrete manufacturing process. Examples of discrete products are automobiles, computers, furniture, and component assemblies.

**Idea to Market for Produced Physical Products (process industry)**

addresses all activities associated with managing the lifecycle of a tangible product that is created using process manufacturing technique, which combines ingredients using formulas and recipes to create products common to food, beverages, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, consumer packaged goods, cannabis, and biotechnology industries.

**Idea to Market for New Vehicle (automotive OEM)**

involves all activities associated with managing the lifecycle of a new vehicle from planning investments, defining requirements, designing vehicle concept to engineering and prototyping before handing it over to manufacturing, while managing all foundational activities associated with developing a new vehicle including project management.

**Source to Pay (S2P)**

*Supply*

Source to Pay includes all activities associated with managing the comprehensive sourcing and procurement of goods and services. It starts with procurement planning and managing spend, followed by sourcing and supplier selection, negotiating and managing supplier contracts, and preparing and executing operational procurement. The next steps are goods receipt, initiating potential returns and claims, processing supplier invoices and accounts payable, including final payment to the supplier, and managing supplier data.

The source-to-pay process varies mainly based on the type of product or service being purchased. Variants of S2P cover, for example, procurement of indirect and direct products, services, and solutions (such as combination of products and services), as well as MRO and field service materials.

Plan to Fulfill Involves all activities related to the planning, production, delivery, and fulfillment of products or services as well as aspects such as tracking and tracing, data management, and sustainable manufacturing operations. Products could be tangible or intangible, such as software. Services could be classic field services, such as installation or maintenance, but could also be professional services or software as a service.

The planning stage of the process involves defining supply chain, manufacturing and service-fulfillment strategies; planning demand, inventory, and supply; aligning plans through sales and operations planning; and finally, managing supply chain performance. The planning process culminates in the initiation of operational procurement.

The production stage of the process includes production planning, production operations, quality management, and production performance management – for both tangible and intangible goods.

The delivery-and-fulfillment stage of the process differs for tangible goods versus services. For tangible goods, it covers inbound or outbound deliveries in any context as well as order promising, warehouse and inventory management, dock and yard logistics, transportation management, and logistics performance management. For services, it involves service planning and scheduling, service execution and delivery, and service performance management.

The process concludes with enabling and foundational activities, such as data management, collaboration, identifying and tracking material, and sustainable manufacturing operations.

Recruit to Retire includes all activities associated with hiring the internal and external workforce and managing their lifecycle in the organization. **Acquire to Decommission (A2D)**

*Corporate*

Acquire to Decommission covers all activities associated with the lifecycle management of assets. This includes planning asset strategy and investments, defining asset maintenance strategies (such as reactive or proactive improvement work), acquiring or building assets, onboarding assets, and planning and executing asset maintenance, as well as offboarding and decommissioning assets. Throughout the lifecycle, the management of asset data and risks can take place.

The A2D process varies mainly based on the type of asset that is managed. Variants of A2D cover, for example, tangible assets, such as serialized devices, equipment, production facilities, and real estate, or intangible assets, such as software or intellectual property.

Governance covers processes related to planning and defining enterprise strategy, establishing and managing sustainable operations, enabling streamlined portfolio and project management operations across various use cases, managing relevant elements of governance, risk, and compliance, international trade, global tax and also managing and operating IT.

**Key Acronyms, Concepts, Definitions and Blogs**

**Key Acronyms**

**Enterprise Domain(s)**

All enterprises can be structured along four **Enterprise Domains** - Products and Services (where Products and Services are managed), Customer (where demand is generated), Supply (where demand is fulfilled) and Corporate (where the Enterprise is managed).. You will see reference to these throughout the presentation and the four colors provide a structure and consistency to the set of eight end-to-end business processes, especially in the area of reusability.

**Business Process Model**

A **set of eight E2E Business Processes** describe the value creation of an enterprise in a standardized way. These processes provide the foundation and building blocks for standardized business process modelling and cover the essential value generating processes of an enterprise. The sections are structured by the individual end-to-end business processes and the user can quickly navigate between processes by utilizing this menu at the top right hand corner of each slide -

**Main Solution Architectures**

The **SAP Reference Solution Architecture** (RSA) represents SAP's recommendation on how the SAP Reference Business Architecture shall be implemented using SAP's integrated solution portfolio to run an 'Intelligent Enterprise’. In this presentation we currently focus on Cloud only with the underlying SAP S/4HANA system being SAP S/4HANA Cloud (Public). In the diagrams we do show various other aspects of the product portfolio supporting the E2E business process and group them into these areas.

**Core Entities in the Methodology**

The Enterprise Architecture Framework (EAF) in this presentation are along the lines of the  E2E Business Process Model and Solution Value flows.
