## Additional Background: Business Capabilities

**General Definition**

Business Capabilities describe the organization's capacity to successfully perform business activities, thereby achieving its business objectives and delivering value to its customers. A capability constitutes “a particular ability or capacity that a business may possess or exchange to achieve a specific purpose or outcome”. Business Capabilities are the core of the business architecture because they define **WHAT** the business does (not how it does it). A business capability can be something that exists today or something that is required to enable a new direction or strategy.

**Creating Business Value**

The concept of the “business capability” has emerged to become a fundamental component in the development of a Business Architecture. As many organizations struggle with managing the complexity of their business, they want to answer questions about their progress toward realizing their strategic aims. Business capabilities provide an abstraction of the business reality in a way that helps to simplify conversations between interested stakeholders, providing an actionable framework for business and IT.

## Business capabilities analysis

**4 Dimensions**

**1st Business readiness:** how ready is the customer to adopt the business changes required by establishing/improving a given capability. For instance the capability fulfillment informs the company on their current strongest and weakest capabilities.

- Business readiness may be evaluated in different ways:

  - efforts and time to reach the capability from the as-is situation,
  - required resources and changes (process, people, assets) to adopt the capability,
  - current fulfillment of targeted capability,
  - business change investment or reluctance,
  - cost of operating the future capability.

- Business readiness score of a given capability show the real change capacity & prio of the customer towards its business objectives

- ***Example:*** *currently the procurement department of company XYZ manages suppliers in a decentral way. This leads to lost procurement deal contracts, administration efforts/overhead and lack of visibility in spent. However the question is how ready is the procurement department of XYZ to manage suppliers centrally? This requires adoption of new processes, change management and new rules e.g. identification of global suppliers, renegotiation of contracts, central governance of suppliers, new workflows and approval processes to be defined and organized and even procurement department org. change.*

## Business capabilities analysis

**4 Dimensions**

**2nd : Capabilities business benefits:** inform on the value impact a capability provides to the company. Companies should invest in differentiating and competitive capabilities (which by definition should have high values) when the current fulfillment is low.

- ***Example:*** *Company XYZ has recently launched a new department to fulfill industry 4.0 digital capabilities for its manufacturing plant. The current business benefits of the digital factory are still low as they are still investing time & efforts in determining the right use cases for connecting the shop floor machines. The business benefit of the capability is therefore low even if the capability itself is considered to be a differentiating one for the company. XYZ therefore continues to invest to bring this capability to a harvest zone.*

**3rd : Capability impact:** informs on the impact of a given capability on the organizational dimension

- Geographic impact (global/regional/local), size of user base, sense of urgency, positive impact on risks

## Business capabilities analysis

**4 Dimensions**

**4th: Capabilities clustering/classification:** how strategic/important is a given capability for the organization

- Capabilities can be classified into differentiating, competitive and base capabilities (see slide 25 for more detailed explanation)

- ***Example:*** *the company XYZ is a chemical industry producing fertilizers for agriculture. Therefore seasonal planning during the year can cause planners an increase in manual effort to get the demand right, in the right place, at the right time. This requires demand forecasting models that need to be adapted to different markets requirements around the world. Company XYZ can potentially loose consequent amount of revenues if the forecast is not correct. Forecasting demand is therefore a huge competitive advantage for XYZ if its products are in the market at the right time. This is a competitive capability for XYZ.*
- *Predicting demand based on intelligent technologies by being connected to farmers & fields represents a differentiating advantage for XYZ*
- *Cleaning the manufacturing plant is part of EHS regulations for XYZ. It is absolutely necessary for the production plant but doesn’t provide a competitive/strategic advantage. Cleaning services (third party procurement) is a base capability for XYZ.*

**Capabilities classification**

- **Core Differentiated:** The Core Differentiated capabilities must be differentiating to the industry average. They should be supported by Own Practices. They should provide strategic value and unique value to the company

  - IT considerations for core differentiating capabilities

    - Leverage strategic vendors, understand vendor strategy & roadmap
    - Invest in best of breed
    - Consider IT as a strategic enabler
    - Prioritize investments
    - Invest on an innovation platform to build own specific capabilities
    - Analyze strengths and weaknesses of IT supports to critical Business Capabilities

- **Core Competitive:** The Core Competitive Capabilities must be at least industry average and providing competitive value (products and services) to their customers and allows business to remain competitive in marketplace. They should be supported by Industry Best Practices.

  - IT considerations for core competitive capabilities

    - Leverage strategic vendors, understand vendor strategy & roadmap
    - Invest in integrated solutions, prioritize investments, optimize ROI
    - Limit the solution risks
    - Reduce SLA`s

- **Base (Non-Core):** Base capabilities are related to low value areas. These should be operated effectively at lowest cost possible. They are usually supported by industry standard practices. Non-core competencies differ from core competencies in two important respects.

- First, they do not help differentiate the organization from the others in the marketplace.

- Second, they do not directly impact the organization’s products and services.

  - IT considerations for base capabilities

    - Leverage strategic vendors, understand vendor strategy & roadmap
    - Leverage standard, optimize ROI
    - Optimize integration
    - Find synergies and duplications: Reduce the nr of solutions, consolidate, rationalize
    - Lower risk, stable core
    - Reduce SLA`s
