## Module Introduction

The purpose of the business strategy map is to provide a cross-organizational reference of how your company meets its strategic priorities in practical terms through goals, value drivers and initiatives. It provides business context for your architecture work.

**Understand Strategy**

Gather the strategic priorities of your company and your organization.

**Associate your initiatives**\
Identify the strategic priorities(s), goal(s), and value driver(s) that are supported by your initiative(s).*

**Understand Goals**\
Gather the goals of your organization and link related value drivers

## Motivation and Benefits of Business Strategy Map

- Helps translate business strategy into business architecture by linking the strategy map to business capabilities.
- Helps gain visibility of business readiness for adoption of  new / improved business changes.
- Helps aligning Business and IT priorities by ensuring the business critical priorities are addressed in the IT Architecture and roadmap design.

**Benefits**

- **Strategic Priority**: A strategic priority is defined as something that creates, motivates, and fuels the change in an organization.
- **Business Goal**: A goal is defined as an end state that a stakeholder intends to achieve.
- **Objective (Value Driver):** Measurable short or mid-term objective having business relevance, as well as target value and schedule.
- **Business Capability**: Business Capabilities describe the organization's ability to successfully perform business activities, achieve its business objectives and deliver value to its customers.

## Artifact – Business Strategy Map

A **business strategy map** is a visual tool that links an organization's strategic priorities, goals, and objectives in a clear and structured way. Strategic priorities define an organization’s key focus areas, goals translate these into specific long-term outcomes, and objectives break them down into measurable, actionable steps. A strategy map is a key artifact in enterprise architecture planning, as it links an organization’s strategy to its architecture.

**Benefits:**

Business Strategy Map

A

**Business Strategy Map**

- Strategic Alignment – It ensures all organizational units and initiatives are aligned with the overall strategy.
- Improved Communication – It provides a clear, visual representation of strategic priorities, making it easier to communicate the strategy across all levels.
- Better Decision-Making – By showing how objectives are interconnected, it supports more informed and coordinated planning and execution.

## Artifact – Business Footprint Diagram

A **Business Footprint Diagram** is a visual tool that integrates strategy, business, solution, and technology architecture domains by aligning strategic objectives with the supporting business and IT operating model. It typically shows organizational units, goals, capabilities, processes, solutions, technologies, and locations—grouped into layers—to provide a comprehensive view of the architecture scope. This helps assess current and future states, supporting strategic planning and transformation initiatives.

**Benefits:**

Business Footprint Diagram

A

**Business Footprint Diagram**

- Strategic Alignment – It links business goals with IT and solution components, ensuring architectural decisions support strategic priorities.
- Holistic Visibility – It provides a comprehensive view across business, solution, and technology domains, helping identify overlaps, gaps, and dependencies.

## Module Scoping

Understand the relevant Business Divisions that are in scope

- a. Is the strategy defined on group/corporate level?
- b. Are there divisional or regional specific strategies?
- c. Is there a difference between group strategy and divisional/regional strategies?
- d. How are the interlinkages between the different strategies in the group?

The main effort driver is the number of distinct strategies that need to be considered. This is captured as driver Business Division (BD).

Examples:

- Strategy is only defined on group level: BD = 1
- Group level strategy but also two distinct divisional strategies: BD = 3
- Group level strategy, on divisional level this strategy is only refined with minor changes: BD = 1

## Scoping Approach – Step 1

Understand the relevant Business Domains that are in scope

- a. Is the strategy further defined by business domains?
- b. Which business domains are in scope for strategy mapping?

The main effort driver is the number of distinct business domains that need to be considered. This is captured as driver Business Domain (LOB).

Examples:

- Strategy is only defined on group level and by business domains, focus of service is HR and Finance: BD = 1, LOB = 2
- Group level strategy but also two distinct divisional strategies including three business domains for each division: BD = 3, LOB = 3
- Group level strategy, on divisional level this strategy is only refined with minor changes, strategy not further specified on business domain level: BD = 1, LOB = 1

## Scoping Approach – Step 2

- Discuss with customer what type of strategy related documents can be shared. Decided if this gives sufficient input for the strategy mapping exercise.
- Note: in some cases, the three levels strategic priorities, goals, and objectives is too detailed for customers especially if the customer does not have a strategy function in the organization. In that case, consider only using priorities and goals.
- If customer is not able to provide any documents (e.g. confidentiality), re-discuss with customer if the strategy map should still be in scope. As a fallback, interviews are always possible to gather the information. However, add to the final effort estimation additional buffer for conducting the interviews and make customer aware that this will increase effort on both sides.
