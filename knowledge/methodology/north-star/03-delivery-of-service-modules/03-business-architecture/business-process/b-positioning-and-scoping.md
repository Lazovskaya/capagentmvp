## Module Introduction

A Business Process combines value-adding activities to achieve a business goal and generate stakeholder value; the business process-driven approach focuses on how value is created, enabling the derivation of business capabilities and product maps, as seen in SAP reference models linking value flows to capabilities.

**Understand Process Scope**

Gain insight into in-scope business value flows based on SAP best practices.

**Perform Heatmapping** \
Assess value flows based to identify pain points, opportunities or standardization potential.

**Identify As-Is and To-Be Applications**\
Understand what applications are in-scope as of today and in future to implement the solution value flow.

## Artifact – Business Value Flow Diagram

A **Business Value Flow diagram** provides a strategic, high-level view of how value-adding activities align with business goals, helping organizations plan enterprise architecture, bridge Business and IT, and identify opportunities for efficiency, innovation, and strategic alignment—complementing the detailed, operational focus of business process models.

**Benefits:**

Business Value Flow Diagram

A

- Provides a clear, strategic overview of value creation across the organization, in contrast to the complexity of detailed process models.
- Visualizes value-adding flows to identify inefficiencies and improvement opportunities across organizational units.
- Helps identify the necessary business capabilities based on how value is created and delivered.

## Artifact – Solution Value Flow Diagram

A **Solution Value Flow Diagram** is a visual representation that illustrates how value is created and propagated across a solution architecture by linking business activities with corresponding solution components and solution capabilities, providing a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms behind value creation and enabling the optimization of business processes and solution architectures.

**Benefits:**

Solution Value Flow Diagram

A

- Strategic Alignment – It ensures all organizational units and initiatives are aligned with the overall strategy.
- Improved Communication – It provides a clear, visual representation of strategic priorities, making it easier to communicate the strategy across all levels.
- Better Decision-Making – By showing how objectives are interconnected, it supports more informed and coordinated planning and execution.

## Module Scoping

- Leverage the SAP RBA content, select the appropriate industry, and define with the customer what business process variants should be in-scope.
- For each E2E process (Lead-to-Cash, Plan-to-Fulfill etc.) try to keep the amount of variants in-scope as low as possible. Focus on the main variants relevant to the customer. Otherwise, the effort will substantially increase by each variant that needs to be covered.
- Understand already what parts of the variants might not be relevant for the customer (e.g. if planning is and will be outside of the SAP landscape, planning might not be a focus topic for the customer).
- Based on the selected variants, derive the number and skill sets of experts that are needed to cover the different parts of the variant (e.g. for Make-to-Stock, you might need PP, TM, EWM, QM etc. experts). Again here, make sure to reduce the number of additional experts needed as much as possible by trying to understand from the customer what parts of the variants are of most importance. Only for these, bring in additional solution experts. Otherwise, the effort would increase significantly.

| Driver | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Business Divisions (BD) | Distinct parts of the business that will need to be covered by the service and will provide distinct stakeholders and participants for the service thereby necessitating effort, analysis and sub deliverables. If there are multiple divisions covered that can be clubbed together from this perspective, please reduce the count appropriately by clubbing them together. |
| Business Process Variant (BPV) | A process variant is a sequence of activities that has a start event and a final event, and differs from other variants by at least one activity. Examples of process variants for a billing process could be Services Billing vs. Materials Billing. |
| Functional Steams (FS) | Identify the number of functionality streams (functional and technical experts) that will be required to deliver the service. Ideally this should be equal to the business domains supported but can differ (for e.g. Finance could require multiple experts for costing, accounting, treasury, etc.) |
